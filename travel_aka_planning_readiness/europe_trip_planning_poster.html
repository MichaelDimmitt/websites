Can I Book Hotels Day-Of?

The Honest Answer

Technically yes. There is no legal requirement to have accommodation pre-booked before entering Spain or France as a US citizen. However — border officers at BCN and CDG can ask for proof of where you're staying, and arriving without it is a flag. The practical answer: have at least your first night booked in Barcelona before you land. After that, you have flexibility. Spain and France in peak summer (June–August) also have genuine inventory pressure — day-of availability exists but quality narrows fast, especially in Barcelona where tourism caps are actively enforced. The rolling horizon rule applies here: lock the skeleton, leave the flesh flexible.

The 90/180 Rule — Know Your Clock

Schengen Time Limit

Spain and France are both Schengen. Your entire trip counts as one block of days against a 90-day allowance within any rolling 180-day window. The EES system now tracks this digitally — no more relying on passport stamps. If you've spent time in any Schengen country in the prior 6 months, those days count. For a typical 2–3 week Barcelona → Madrid → France trip you are well within limits. The risk is only relevant if this is part of a longer stretch of Europe travel.
