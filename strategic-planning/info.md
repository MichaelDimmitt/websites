Travel

Flights: 1–3 months out is the sweet spot for domestic; 3–6 months for international. Too early and prices haven't dropped yet; too late and they spike.
Hotels: 1–2 months for popular destinations, though last-minute deals exist for flexible travelers.
Peak seasons (holidays, summer): push everything 6+ months out.

Events (weddings, concerts, big gatherings)

Venues: 6–12 months is standard; top venues book a year+ in advance.
Vendors (caterers, photographers): 3–6 months.
Casual gatherings: 2–4 weeks is usually plenty, but more lead time = better RSVPs.

Appointments

Doctors/specialists: 2–6 weeks for routine; same-week for urgent.
Popular services (dentist, dermatologist, hair): 2–4 weeks.
High-demand providers: sometimes 2–3 months, so book your next one before you leave the current one.

General Life Planning

1–3 months out: Ideal for most personal logistics — registrations, reservations, prep work.
The "2-week window": Great for confirming plans, following up, and handling details.
Weather: Only reliable within 7–10 days, so don't lock in outdoor plans based on forecasts further out than that.

A good rule of thumb: the harder something is to change or the more other people depend on it, the earlier you should lock it in. Travel and events fall squarely in that category.

1. Travel
The hard events on your calendar are actually your biggest asset here. Work backwards from them:

Lock in return travel the moment you know a hard deadline exists.
Then plan outbound with flexibility, knowing your constraint is the return.
This also protects you from the most expensive scenario — last-minute one-way tickets home.

2. Health Appointments — Yes, Seasonality Applies

January–February is actually the worst time for availability. New year resolutions, new insurance kicking in, and people who deferred holiday appointments all flood in at once.
Late summer (August–September) has a similar crunch before school/fall schedules lock in.
Best windows: March–May and October–November tend to have the most flexibility.
The pro move: book your next appointment before you leave the current one. You get first pick of the schedule before it fills.

3. Hard Calendar Events as an Anchor System
This is the key insight — your fixed events are the skeleton. Everything else should be scheduled relative to them:

Travel buffers before and after
Health appointments in the calmer stretches between events
Big purchases or projects in low-stress windows
